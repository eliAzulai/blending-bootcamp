1
Now · weeks

Pilot — prove kids come back

Small circle, tight scope, real answers to the motivation question.

  • A pet kids actually care about — visible, reactive, gets hungry if they skip a day
  • Three activities only — phonics, read‑aloud, spelling. Nothing else, on purpose
  • Daily 7–10 min session — picked automatically from each kid's focus areas
  • Your dashboard — who practiced, what they did, this week
  • You review read‑alouds when you have time — AI never grades the kid
Gate to Phase 2 60% of pilot kids practice 4+ days per week for 4 weeks in a row — and you say "yes, this works."

  • More activity types — writing prompts, comprehension, sight words, story listening
  • AI‑generated content — endless passages & word lists in the style you want
  • Teacher content uploads — use your own materials, not just the seeded library
Gate to Phase 3 Pilot expands beyond your current cohort — other parents & (maybe) other teachers ask to use it.

Business — turn it into income

Your hardest question — answer #23. Phase 3 is the only honest reply I have.

It won't make money and we will continue to be in dire straits, and it will take up my time when I could be teaching privately instead.

Three paths to real income
1 · Your current circle

Bundle WordPets into your teaching fee, or parents subscribe separately. Works with families who already trust you — the easiest "yes" we'll ever get.

Low risk Small ceiling
2 · Students you can't reach today

WordPets becomes your top‑of‑funnel. New parents find it, sign up, some convert to paid live lessons with you. This is the one that grows your teaching income.

Medium risk Middle ceiling
3 · Other teachers

Other English teachers license WordPets for their own students — you're the anchor case. Product income, not teaching income. A different career shape.

High risk Biggest ceiling
What this would mean for you — three futures to pick from
A · Stay a teacher with a great tool. Path 1 only. No business change, no founder life. WordPets is the reason your students stick and your parents pay a bit more.
B · Teacher‑founder. Paths 1 + 2. You keep teaching but WordPets brings new students to you. Fewer hours for the same or better income — your ask #15, delivered.
C · Step back from live teaching. All three paths. The product earns. You teach when you want, not because you have to. Biggest upside, biggest change to your daily life.
What Phase 3 actually decides
  • Pricingsubscription, per‑student, or bundled into your fee
  • Customerdirect‑to‑parent, via other teachers, or through schools
  • Opennessprivate beta, public launch, or open‑source
  • Teamthe two of us, or we bring someone in
The honest risks
  • Parents might not pay. Your current parents are friends — paying strangers behave differently.
  • Other teachers might not want it. They could see it as competition, not a tool. Path 3 would stall.
  • You might prefer teaching. If founder life drains you, we stop at Phase 2. That's a full, good ending — not a failure.
  • This is a direction, not a deadline. Phase 3 only begins when Phase 2 is stable and real signals — not vibes — say go.

What this roadmap is and isn't

It's a compass, not a promise. If the pilot shows kids don't come back for the pet, we don't paper over it and march to Phase 2 — we rework the pet first. If Phase 2 works but nothing about a "business" feels right to you, we stop at Phase 2 and just keep teaching with it. The three phases are gates, not guarantees. Your honest reaction at each one is what decides the next.
