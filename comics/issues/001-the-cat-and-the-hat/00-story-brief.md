# 00 Story Brief — The Cat and the Hat

> Worked example. This brief was reconstructed from the approved pilot to
> calibrate `prompts/story-interview.md`: it shows the *output* a good
> Hook/Heart/Joke interview should produce, at or above the pilot's bar.

## Issue
- Stage: cvc-short-a
- Slug: the-cat-and-the-hat
- Working title: The Cat and the Hat

## Word budget
- Primary target words (My Words): Sam, cat, hat, mat
- Other allowed drill words: bat, pan, man (use only if they earn it — none did)
- Sight words available: the, a, is, I, have, see, on, and, can, look, no, yes
- Cast available at this stage: Sam; the cat (Whiskers — name gated to a later
  stage, so "cat" / "the cat" only)

## The three parts
- **Hook:** Sam holds a straw hat so big it's almost bigger than Sam — a hat
  nobody could actually wear right.
- **Heart:** Sam wanted the hat to be *his* thing, and gives it up gladly the
  moment it makes the cat happy.
- **Joke:** The cat claims the squashed hat as the world's best bed and won't be
  moved — the hat finally has an owner, just not the one anyone expected.
  - Twist (why a kid won't expect it): the obvious beat is "cat sits on hat"; the
    real payoff is the cat treating the wrecked hat as a *prize*, so the
    accident becomes the happy ending.

## Beat spine (6-9 beats)
1. Sam holds the comically oversized hat. "I have a hat."
2. Sam sets the hat on the mat to look at it. "The hat is on the mat."
3. The cat creeps in from the side and notices the hat. (wordless)
4. The cat studies the hat while Sam watches. (wordless)
5. The cat gets on the hat; it squashes flat. "The cat is on the hat!"
6. The cat settles in, blissfully content on the ruined hat. (wordless)
7. Sam laughs and gently pats the cat. "I see the cat."
8. Sam sits on the mat beside the cat. "Sam is on the mat."
9. Final: the cat asleep on the hat, Sam dozing nearby in afternoon sun. (wordless)

## Why this isn't the obvious version
The bland version is "Sam has a hat; the cat sits on it; the end" — an event, not
a joke. We kept the same four words but loaded the hook (a hat too big for
*anyone*) and pushed the payoff past the literal sit: the cat *wants* the
squashed hat, which turns Sam's small loss into a warm, funny resolution. No new
content word was needed to get there.

## Risks / words to verify in the validator
- "have", "see", "on" used as sight words — confirm against the stage allowlist

- No drill words (bat/pan/man) used, so no extra decodability risk introduced.
