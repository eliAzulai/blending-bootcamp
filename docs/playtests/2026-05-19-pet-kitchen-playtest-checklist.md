# Pet Kitchen Playtest Checklist

**Date:** 2026-05-19  
**Status:** Historical/deferred — Pet Kitchen is not current implementation scope as of 2026-05-27  
**Target:** WordPets Phase 1a, ages 6-8  
**Session length:** 5-10 minutes  
**Tester:** Ilana, Eli, or parent watching quietly

> Do not use this as an active playtest checklist unless Eli explicitly
> re-scopes Pet Kitchen and the feature exists again in the current
> `/student/practice` app.

## Setup

- Open `/student/fixture-student-1`.
- Do not explain the game at first.
- Let the child tap around for 30-60 seconds.
- Only help if the child is stuck or frustrated.

## What To Watch

### First 30 Seconds

- Does the child notice **Pet Kitchen**?
- Does the child understand that this is the next thing to tap?
- Does the child seem curious, neutral, or resistant?

### First Word

- Does the child understand they should listen to the word?
- Does the child understand the letters go into the tray?
- Does the child connect spelling the word with cooking the snack?
- Does the pet reaction feel noticeable?

### Mistakes

- If the child makes a mistake, do they know how to try again?
- Does the hint help?
- Does the child feel corrected or embarrassed?
- Does the child keep going?

### Reward Loop

- Does the child notice the snack cooking?
- Does the child notice the pet eating/reacting?
- Does the coin reward matter to them?
- Do they want to do the next snack?

### Learning Signal

- Are mistakes real spelling struggles or random tapping?
- Which letters/sounds cause hesitation?
- Does the child say the word aloud or silently build it?
- Does the child use sound order, or just visual guessing?

## Ask Afterward

Ask casually, not like a quiz:

1. "What were you doing in the kitchen?"
2. "What happened when you spelled the word?"
3. "Was anything confusing?"
4. "Do you want to cook another snack?"
5. "What snack should the pet get next?"

## Quick Ratings

Mark each from 1-5:

- Child understood the task: `1 2 3 4 5`
- Child wanted to continue: `1 2 3 4 5`
- Pet reward felt motivating: `1 2 3 4 5`
- Mistakes felt safe: `1 2 3 4 5`
- Spelling stayed central: `1 2 3 4 5`
