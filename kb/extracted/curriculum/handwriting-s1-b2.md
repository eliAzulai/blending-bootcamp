Tips to Teach Handwriting
1.  Keep handwriting sessions short and positive. Learn one new letter a day. Stop if the 
child is gets too tired or their work gets too sloppy. Go and do something else for a 
bit. Come back later or the next day fresh with new energy. We suggest trying to do 
one page of a new letter a day and the corresponding activity (if there is one for that 
letter).
2.  Handwriting is about accuracy and developing correct habits—not about speed. 
Remember that one single nice letter is better for the child’s progress than pages of 
sloppy letters. 
3.  Practice hand and upper body strength outside of handwriting time. The stronger 
their hands, arms, shoulders, and core are, the better their handwriting will be. Do 
lots of playdough, cutting with scissors, crawling on hands and knees, and using 
clothespins.
4.  Always have the child sit down in a chair at a table to do handwriting. Sit in a chair 
next to the child at a table. Don’t sit across from the child or on a sofa. 
5.  Teach about Cam the Cat’s position (explained more on page 6). For each letter, ask “Is 
this letter going to start at the top of Cam’s head or body, or go down to his tail?” Use 
the Cam the Cat puppet on a pencil or a popsicle stick as a fun reminder. The Cam the 
Cat puppet can also be a great way to measure the space between words. 
6.  Show the child how to form the letter before they pick up a pencil. Demonstrate 
with a pencil or your finger the steps to building a new letter. Describe what you are 
4

## Page 5

doing as you do it. For example: “Start at the top, go straight down, and then cross 
at the top.”
7.  Follow these steps to teach the formation of the letter: 
1.  Have the child trace each step when building a new letter with just their  
finger after seeing you do it first. 
2.  Have the child trace each step of building the new letter with their pencil  
or marker. 
3.  Have the child follow the steps for practicing each letter on the page. 
4.  Have the child follow up with the corresponding activity. 
8.  Circle the best letter or word your child does on each page. We always focus on the 
positive. Give exact reasons why the letter is their best one. For example: “Look! You 
started at the top of that A so well!” Or: “I see you really got that S to sit right on the 
ground!” Give a star when the page is completed. 
9.  Simply correct the child if they are forming the words incorrectly. Avoid saying “No” 
or anything very negative. Smile and say something like: “Watch how I do it again, 
and then try that.” Demonstrate the correct way and have the child try it while you 
supervise. Frequently point out what the child does right. 
10.  This book introduces the letters that are taught in set 1 of our Dash into Learning 
early reader books. Using these coordinating resources at the same time rein-
forces the child’s learning by teaching them to write and read the letters and sounds 
together. Just as the 3 sets of early reader books complete our early reading program, 
the 3 corresponding handwriting books completes our handwriting program.

Tell the child: “Cam the Cat LOVES writing and wants to help! Cam reminds us which sec-
tion of the line we should write each letter on.” Then point to the cat and say: “Each letter 
matches a certain part of Cam the Cat. Some letters match his head, some his body, and 
some his tail.” Now point to the bottom line, or baseline, and say: “If the letter sits just on 
the baseline, it matches Cam’s body.” Slide your finger up to the top line and say: “If the let-
ter goes all the way to the top line, it matches his head and body.” Pointing below the base-
line, say: “If it goes below the baseline, it matches Cam’s tail.” 
Cam the Cat can help as a puppet too! Cut out the puppet and glue it to the end of a small 
popsicle stick or pencil for easier use. You can place it on any lines the child is writing to 
help them remember to stay in Cam’s matching sections on the lines. You can also place the 
puppet between words as a spacing measurement.

Cam the Cat Pencil Puppet
Our Cam the cat pencil puppet is a fun way to remind your child to write correctly. Color and  
cut out Cam the Cat and slice along the two straight lines.  Weave the pencil in and out so it  
stays at the end of the pencil.  Tell the child:  “Cam the cat wants to write with you!  Every time  
you see him, remember to leave room for him on your line and put your letter in the right place.  See 
how long you can write with him on your pencil!”
