# Video-Book Hybrid Format

The most engaging reading content for young children is neither a static book nor a full video — it's something in between: a slideshow with audio, simple movement, and humor.

## What Kids vs Phonics does

They create short videos that are essentially animated storybooks:
- Static illustrations with minimal movement
- Narrated audio (simple, silly stories)
- Stories use ONLY the sounds/letters the child has already learned
- Humor and absurdity make it memorable ("Dip It is a ridiculous word, it doesn't even mean anything, it's just the name of the robot")

## Why it works

The constraint of using only known sounds creates inherently funny, bizarre sentences. A child who only knows /a/, /n/, /d/, /i/ can only read words like "and," "in," "an," "Dan," "Dad" — so the stories are absurd by necessity, and kids find this hilarious.

## What Ilana wants to reproduce

"What's really cool about it that I like and would want to reproduce in my own is that they make a little video which isn't intended to be like an actual dynamic video necessarily but it's more like something in between a video and a book."

## Application to WordPets

The Read Aloud activity could adopt this format:
- Animated illustrations (pet characters in silly scenarios)
- Audio narration that the child follows along with
- Stories constrained to only use sounds/words the child has learned
- The absurdity is a feature, not a bug — lean into it

## Source evaluation

Ilana liked the video format a lot but was mixed on the actual books: "The book isn't so amazing... it's just a phonics, it's just like a little bit silly." The format is what to steal, not necessarily the content quality.
