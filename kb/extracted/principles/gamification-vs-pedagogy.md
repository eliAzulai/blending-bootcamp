# Gamification vs. Pedagogy — Steal the Wrapper, Not the Teaching

Teach Your Monster to Read has excellent game design (journey through lands, character progression, Fortnite-like adventure feel) but weak pedagogy. Borrow the engagement wrapper, build better teaching underneath.

## What to steal

- Character walking through different lands (progression feels like an adventure)
- Video-game style graphics and animations
- Story-driven progression (not just "do 10 more words")
- The sense of journey and exploration

## What NOT to copy

"I actually do not think is a good program in terms of like pedagogically, in terms of learning, but I like the video game style." The teaching methodology itself — how they present sounds, practice blending, assess understanding — is not strong.

## Application to WordPets

The pet system already serves as the engagement layer. The principle here is: keep the game/reward mechanics separate from the teaching mechanics. The pet (game layer) should motivate practice, but the actual phonics/spelling activities (teaching layer) should follow the proven principles from Letterbooks, Whitebooklets, and ABC See Hear Do.

Don't let game design decisions compromise teaching quality. If a game mechanic makes the teaching worse (e.g., rushing through sounds to earn more coins), remove the game mechanic, not the teaching.
