# Multisensory Letter Introduction

New letters should be introduced through multiple senses, not just visual recognition. The Letterbook series uses visual, auditory, kinesthetic, and craft-based approaches in sequence.

## The Letterbook sequence for each letter

1. **Visual introduction** — large letter with themed illustration (D for Dog, big D and little d)
2. **Discriminating activity** — which is the "best" dog? (judgment/comparison using the letter's theme)
3. **Writing practice** — trace and write the letter
4. **Listening activity** — identify similarities and differences (both clothes but one is a dress, one is trousers)
5. **Categorization** — sort items by beginning letter (drag desk to D, anteater to A)
6. **Cognitive skills** — size matching, odd-one-out (big dog → big house)
7. **Story + comprehension** — fairy tale or nursery rhyme with simple questions
8. **Craft** — hands-on creative activity

## Why multi-sense matters

"They're great for foundations and beginner readers — they use all the senses, they're fun, they're cute." Children who struggle with purely visual letter recognition often succeed when the letter is connected to movement, sound, stories, and tactile creation.

## Application to WordPets

While an app can't replicate all senses, the phonics activity for each new letter should hit at least 3 modalities:
- Visual: letter card with themed illustration
- Auditory: sound pronunciation + word examples
- Interactive: drag-and-sort or tap-to-match activities
- Reward: pet-related celebration tied to the letter's theme

## Missing piece

Ilana noted the instructions for some activities were at the back of the book, not embedded in the pages: "The instructions are at the end which is kind of annoying but it should be embedded into the program." In the app, instructions must be inline — never separate.
