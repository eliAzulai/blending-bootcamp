# Consistency Breeds Trust

Decodable readers must maintain identical structure across books so kids know exactly what to expect. The Whitebooklet series demonstrates this perfectly.

## The consistency contract

- Every book has the exact same number of pages (7 pages early on, 15 pages later)
- Same recurring characters appear across multiple books (the two dogs in books 31-33)
- Word review page always appears at the back
- Sight words always marked with asterisk
- New high-frequency words listed before the story begins

## Why it matters

"Each book needs to be exact same amount of pages so it's familiar so the kids know the consistency." When a child picks up book 32, they already know the format from books 1-31. This removes cognitive load from the reading experience — they can focus entirely on decoding, not on figuring out how the book works.

## Progressive difficulty within the same format

The format stays the same but content scales:
- Books 1-10: 7 pages, very few words per page, minimal sentences
- Books 30+: 15 pages (almost double), many more words, full sentences
- Words introduced in early books reappear in later ones

## Application to WordPets

Activities should have a predictable, repeating structure. The child should never wonder "what do I do here?" The UI layout, flow, and feedback patterns must be identical across sessions. Increase difficulty by adding more words or harder patterns, not by changing the interaction model.
