# Movement-Linked Recall (TPR)

Each phoneme sound should be associated with a specific body movement. This Total Physical Response (TPR) approach creates muscle-memory anchors for sounds that children struggle to recall abstractly.

## How it works

ABC See Hear Do assigns a unique movement to every letter sound:
- /k/ (cat) → curl hands into claws and scratch
- /a/ (ant) → throw hands in the air like scared
- /t/ (turtle) → tap in the air with finger

## Why it's powerful

When a child can't remember a sound during reading, the teacher makes the movement gesture. The child sees it and immediately recalls: "oh right, that's /t/!" The physical motion bypasses the abstract letter-sound connection and goes straight to embodied memory.

## Classroom evidence

"Sometimes we'll do with a child in class who can't remember that the sound is /t/ — turtle, tap in the air — so I will just make the tapping motion with my finger and I'll look at them and they'll be like 'oh right, that's /t/' and then they'll remember."

## Application to WordPets

This is hard to fully replicate in an app (no body tracking), but we can:
- Show animated movement cues alongside letter sounds
- Use the character animations as the "movement" equivalent
- When a child gets stuck, show the movement hint animation before revealing the answer
- Consider optional video demonstrations parents can watch with kids

## Note on blending quality

Ilana noted that ABC See Hear Do does individual letter sounds very well but "I don't think they do that great of a job with blending to be honest." Their strength is single-sound recall, not combining sounds into words.
